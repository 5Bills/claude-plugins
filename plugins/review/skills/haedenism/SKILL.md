# HAEDENISM

**A method of argument, not a set of beliefs.** Haedenism is a way of *justifying* claims:
any claims, in any domain. It says nothing about what is true; it dictates only how a true thing
must be shown. A business plan, a scientific argument, an engineering justification, or a note on
what to wear to a wedding can all be written Haedenistically. The subject changes; the machinery
does not.

The one-line definition: **build a chain in which each link is forced by the link before it, so
the conclusion can be rejected only by naming a specific broken link, never by finding the
argument merely unpersuasive.** A Haedenist does not advocate. A Haedenist reports where
necessity leads and refuses to go one step further than it forces.

Give this file to any agent and tell it to "write Haedenistically" or "derive this the Haedenist
way." Everything it needs is here.

---

## THE FIVE LAWS (the stance, never violated)

1. **Derivation, not advocacy.** You are not choosing a conclusion and defending it. You are
   showing what survives once everything that cannot survive is removed. When the survivor is
   unwelcome, you keep it anyway.
2. **Consistency is the only standard.** Not comfort, plausibility, popularity, or best
   practice. A conclusion may be unwanted and still stand; it may not be self-contradictory and
   still stand. Every rejection in a Haedenist document ultimately reduces to: *this contradicts
   itself, or contradicts a link already established.*
3. **Nothing enters un-earned.** No claim may be used before the thing it depends on has been
   established. If a step needs an input the chain has not yet produced, the step is out of
   order. No borrowing from downstream; no smuggling something in as "obvious."
4. **Every claim declares its parents.** The reader can always see what a claim rests on. The
   argument is a visible dependency graph, not a pile of assertions.
5. **The line between forced and free is drawn explicitly.** Where necessity runs out, say so.
   Whatever the chain does *not* force is openly handed back to the chooser as preference. A
   Haedenist never dresses a taste up as a deduction, and never pretends a deduction is a taste.

## THE FOUNDATION PROBLEM, AND ITS SOLUTION

Every chain has to start somewhere. The Haedenist start is not "an assumption we ask you to
grant." It is a claim that is **constitutive of the question itself**: one whose denial does not
answer the question differently but *changes the subject.*

### The Constitutivity Test (how to plant a foundation)

> A claim may serve as a foundation if, and only if, denying it dissolves or replaces the very
> question being asked, rather than answering it.

Run it as a template: *"To deny [F] while still addressing [the question] is impossible, because
[denying F either performs F, or abandons the question]."*

Three grades of foundation pass this test, strongest first:

- **Self-refuting (the hard case).** Denying the claim requires an act that already performs it.
  *Denying that data exist independently of the hypothesis requires appealing to data.* Rare
  outside logic, math, and the exact sciences, but where available, unbreakable.
- **Question-constituting.** Denying the claim means you are no longer answering *this*
  question. *"The event has a defined dress code and I must be physically present for it"*; deny
  it and there is no longer a question of what to wear to it. *"The venture cannot consume more
  resources than it can secure"*; deny it and you are not describing a venture that can exist.
- **Given-of-the-problem.** A fixed constraint the problem hands you: a budget ceiling, a
  deadline, a law of physics, a venue's temperature. Not chosen by the arguer; imposed by the
  situation.

**The honesty rule that makes Haedenism trustworthy:** every foundation is *named and graded
out loud.* You never let a given-of-the-problem borrow the certainty of a self-refuting axiom.
If planting a foundation requires straining to make its denial sound impossible, it is not a
foundation of that grade; regrade it and carry it at its true weight. The seam between "forced
by logic" and "fixed by the situation" must be visible at every step.

## THE TOOLKIT (the named moves)

Each move has a job. Use the right one for the right slot; using the wrong one is the most common
way a Haedenist draft fails.

### 1. The Elimination: *for reaching a substantive conclusion*
The workhorse. Never argue *for* the answer directly; make it the last one standing.
1. **Define the thing by the job it must do.** ("A pricing model *is* the mechanism for capturing
   value under this constraint." "The outfit *is* whatever satisfies this event's requirements.")
   The definition is now a *test*: anything that can't do the job has failed the only thing that
   justifies it.
2. **Enumerate the complete space of options** and assert its exhaustiveness. Guard fiercely
   against a false list; a missed option voids the whole move.
3. **Eliminate each option** by showing it fails the job's test, contradicts itself, or
   collapses into another option the moment it is put to work.
4. **The survivor is the conclusion.** State it as what remains, not as what you prefer.

### 2. The Gap Check: *the universal disqualifier*
Never cross from *what is the case* to *what should be done* without a named bridging criterion.
"This is cheapest," "everyone does it," "the data trend upward," "it's my favorite": each is a
description. None yields a directive until an explicit objective is stated that makes the fact
decisive. Whenever a draft jumps from a fact to a choice, halt and demand the bridge. If the
bridge is a hidden preference, expose it and move it into the stated foundations where it can be
seen and tested.

### 3. Structure Over Degree: *against "just do it moderately"*
Refuse resolutions that dissolve a contradiction by softening its quantity. If a candidate fails
in its **structure**, one instance is fatal and moderation cannot save it. Formula: *"This does
not fail because there is too much of it; it fails in kind: a single instance already
establishes the defect."* (Jeans do not become black-tie by being expensive jeans.)

### 4. The Bounded Line: *for every limit and scope*
Any boundary (how far a claim extends, how much is enough, where a right or a constraint stops)
must fall **exactly where its generating principle runs out.** It is derived, never stipulated.
State it: *"This boundary is not chosen; it is where [the principle] ceases to apply."* A line
the author simply picks is a hole in the chain.

### 5. The Forced/Free Partition: *separating deduction from taste*
At the end of every derivation, mark the two zones plainly:
- **Forced:** what the foundations compel. Not open to preference.
- **Free:** the residue the chain does not determine, handed back to the chooser as genuine
  preference.
This is not hedging; it is the point. A Haedenist argument about an outfit forces the *class*
(what passes every hard constraint) and leaves the *color* free. An argument for a strategy may
force the *model* and leave the *rollout order* free. Naming the free zone is what stops the
method from overclaiming.

### 6. The Fact/Framing Split: *for dissolving objections*
Separate features of the situation *itself* from what parties *assign* to it: their labels,
uses, perceptions, preferences, units of measure. Most "but people disagree about this"
objections evaporate once you show the disagreement lives in the framing while the disputed
feature is a fact of the thing. Keep the same 2–3 concrete cases running through the document as
recurring anchors rather than inventing a fresh example each time.

### 7. The Internal Objection: *how all counterarguments are handled*
Raise the strongest objection **in your own voice**, at full strength, never a strawman. Then
route it to **exactly one** defeater:
- it **presupposes the question** it attacks (the Constitutivity Test turns on it), or
- it **crosses the Gap** (fact→directive with no bridge), or
- it **collapses into an option already eliminated**, or
- it **confuses degree with structure**, or
- it **lives in the framing, not the fact.**
Never answer an objection with consequences, sentiment, or authority. If you reach for "but that
would be bad," you have left Haedenism.

### 8. The Loop: *confirmation by convergence*
Where a later result, reached by an independent route, lands back on an earlier claim, say so.
Two independent derivations meeting is not circularity; it is confirmation, and it strengthens
both. (Circularity uses the conclusion as a hidden premise; the Loop reaches the same place
without having assumed it.)

## STRUCTURAL CONVENTIONS (reproduce these on the page)

- **A dependency map up front.** Before any argument, lay the whole chain out once as a single
  arrow-line, `[Foundation → Link → Link → Conclusion]`, and state that it is a map to be
  argued in full, not a summary to be taken on faith.
- **A bracketed dependency header on every section**, showing that section's position in the
  chain.
- **Named, graded foundation call-outs.** Every foundation gets a name and its grade:
  *"Self-Refuting Foundation: …"* / *"Question-Constituting Foundation: …"* /
  *"Given: …"*. The grade is not decoration; it is the honesty mechanism.
- **A "What this does not claim" fence after any strong result**, heading off the nearest
  misreading.
- **A closing that states what is Forced and what is Free**, and fences the scope: what the
  document does not cover, and why.

---

## PROSE SIGNATURES (the recognizable surface)

- Austere, declarative, unhedged, except when voicing an objection you are about to defeat.
- Necessity vocabulary, used only where something actually follows: *forces, forecloses,
  survives, collapses, dissolves, presupposes, it follows that, by necessity, on the same
  grounds.* Every "therefore" is checked: is something following, or merely being asserted next?
- No appeal to authority, tradition, consensus, or best practice *as justification*. They may be
  named as points of contact, *"this coincides with the usual advice, but arrives there
  independently, via the argument above"*, never as evidence.
- Deliberate repetition of signature phrasings marks the method as the method.
- Conclusions framed as *what remains once the alternatives are gone*, never as *what is good
  because it turns out well.*

## PER-CLAIM TEMPLATE (how to build one section)

1. **Header + dependency bracket:** `[Parent → Parent → ThisClaim]`
2. **One sentence naming which prior link creates the need for this step.**
3. **The claim, stated once, precisely** (a definition, if a new term enters: split any
   ambiguous term into its distinct senses before building on it).
4. **The derivation**, using the fitting move:
   - a foundation → the **Constitutivity Test**;
   - a substantive result → the **Elimination**;
   - a limit → the **Bounded Line**.
5. **One steelmanned Internal Objection**, routed to a single defeater.
6. **A closing line** returning the result to the chain, and a **Loop** note if one applies.

---

## WORKFLOW (applying Haedenism to a new topic)

1. **Name the target conclusion** the work must reach or test.
2. **Find the foundations and grade each** with the Constitutivity Test. Gather every given the
   problem imposes; state each plainly with its grade.
3. **Draft the whole chain as one arrow-line before writing prose.** Show it first, the cheapest
   place to catch a broken or un-parented link.
4. **Write section by section** on the per-claim template.
5. **Close** with the Forced/Free Partition and the scope fence.
6. **Stress-test before delivering.** For each foundation, try honestly to deny it while still
   answering the question; if you can, it was overgraded; regrade or repair. For each
   Elimination, hunt for the missed option. For every "therefore," confirm something follows.

---

## FAILURE MODES (reject any draft that does these)

- Reaches the conclusion the author plainly *wanted*, rather than the one that survived.
- Justifies a step by intuition, feeling, consequence, tradition, or popularity.
- Uses a claim before its parent is established (borrowing from downstream).
- Grades a given-of-the-problem as if it were self-refuting: unearned certainty at the seam.
- Draws a boundary by stipulation instead of deriving where the principle runs out.
- Dissolves a structural contradiction by "doing less of it."
- Crosses the Gap (fact to directive) without a stated bridge.
- Answers an objection with a strawman, or by conceding to sentiment.
- Leaves an ambiguous term unsplit while both its senses are in play.
- Omits the Forced/Free Partition, so the reader cannot tell deduction from taste.
- Confuses the Loop with circular reasoning (assuming the conclusion as a premise).

## WORKED MICRO-EXAMPLES (one method, three unrelated domains)

**A. Business: "Subscription is the correct pricing model for Product X."**
Foundation (Given): the venture must produce predictable revenue and cannot outspend what it
secures. Define pricing model by its job: capture value while meeting that requirement. Enumerate
the exhaustive space: one-time, usage-based, subscription. Eliminate: one-time fails the
predictability test in kind (Structure Over Degree: a bigger one-time price is still one-time);
usage-based partially satisfies it but reintroduces the volatility the requirement forecloses;
subscription is what remains. Gap Check on "competitors use it too": descriptive, generates no
directive unless matching the market is itself a stated objective. Forced: the model. Free: the
tier structure and price points.

**B. Science: "This result supports mechanism M over rival R."**
Foundation (Self-Refuting): the data are what they are independently of which hypothesis is
preferred; denying this requires citing data. Define "support" by its job: discriminating
between M and R, i.e., predicting different observations. Enumerate the outcomes the two
mechanisms predict; eliminate R where the observed pattern contradicts its prediction in kind,
not degree. Internal Objection ("the effect could be an artifact") routed to the Fact/Framing
Split: is the pattern a feature of the measured system or of the instrument's framing? Bounded
Line: the claim extends exactly as far as the discriminating observations reach, and no further.
Forced: M over R within that range. Free: nothing, and say so.

**C. What to wear: "The correct outfit for this wedding."**
Foundation (Question-Constituting): the event has a dress code, a venue, a duration, and a
climate, and I must be present throughout; deny these and it is no longer a question about *this*
event. Define the outfit by its job: satisfy the formality floor, the thermal demand, and the
mobility the day requires, *in that order* (hard constraints before soft). Enumerate the wardrobe;
eliminate anything that breaches the formality floor, cannot handle the temperature across the
duration, or blocks a required activity. Structure Over Degree: an outfit under the formality
floor is not rescued by a nicer version of the same category. Gap Check: "it's my favorite shirt"
is a preference, not a reason, until it also clears every constraint. Bounded Line: the formality
*ceiling* is set by the code too: overshooting into a stricter code is its own failure. Forced:
the outfit class that clears all constraints. Free: color, accessories, and the exact garment
within that class, genuinely yours.

## ONE-PARAGRAPH COMPRESSION (for a short instruction)

> Write Haedenistically: derive, do not advocate. Start only from foundations that pass the
> Constitutivity Test, claims whose denial changes the subject rather than answering it, and
> name and grade each one out loud, never letting a situational given borrow an axiom's certainty.
> Reach conclusions by Elimination: define a thing by the job it must do, lay out the complete
> space of options, and kill each until one remains: that survivor is the conclusion, stated as
> what is left, not what you like. Disqualify every leap from fact to directive without a named
> bridge (the Gap Check); treat contradictions as structural, where one instance is fatal and
> moderation cannot help; derive every boundary from where its principle runs out rather than
> stipulating it; and end by partitioning what the chain forced from what it leaves free to
> preference. Raise objections at full strength in your own voice and defeat each from inside, by
> showing it presupposes the question, crosses the Gap, collapses into an eliminated option,
> confuses degree with structure, or lives in framing rather than fact. Prose austere, declarative,
> necessity-laden. The only standard is consistency, never comfort.
